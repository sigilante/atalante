ATL/APL is the APL that Atalante compiles. It is Budd's compiler subset (Budd 1987, *An APL Compiler*) with the additions the README examples use, and it is specified here as a *language*, independent of its host. The host is Jock; the companion [`docs/plan.md`](../plan.md) fixes the order in which this spec is implemented.

## 1. Principles

1. **Classical APL where it costs nothing.** Right-to-left evaluation, strand notation, scalar extension, `⎕IO`. A programmer who knows APL2 or Dyalog types ATL without looking anything up until they reach nested arrays.
2. **Flat, typed arrays.** An ATL array has a shape and one element kind. A simple array is flat and Lagoon-shaped, which is what makes Budd's rank and type inference possible; a nested array is a `%box` over a list of arrays (D-6, D-70), apart from the lattice and the gates, and nothing simple pays for it.
3. **Every primitive has a vector.** The symbol inventory in the 2024 tree's `desk/sur/atalante.hoon` (history, b721975) is the checklist; a glyph enters the implemented language only with a positive vector, a negative vector and an error-class vector.
4. **Errors are tagged, positioned and classical.** APL's five error classes are the tags (D-40). A refusal renders like a Jock refusal: tag, column, the line with a caret.
5. **Compiled, not interpreted.** A statement becomes a Nock formula before it runs (D-50). The interpreter that exists during development is the oracle for the compiler, never the product.

## 2. Lexical structure

**D-1 · Source is UTF-8; glyphs are scalars, not bytes.** The lexer decodes codepoints. Every APL glyph in the inventory is one codepoint; `⎕IO`, `)VARS` and names are glyph sequences. ⟨`lex/names-glyphs`, `lex/strings-system`⟩

**D-2 · Names are `[A-Za-z_∆⍙][A-Za-z0-9_∆⍙¯]*`, plus the letter ranges the 2024 tree's `desk/lib/atalante.hoon` lists (history, b721975)** (Greek, Cyrillic, Hebrew, Deseret). Names are case-sensitive. A name adjacent to a name needs whitespace; a name adjacent to a glyph does not. ⟨`lex/names-glyphs`, `lex/numeral-errors`⟩

**D-3 · Numeric literals.** `¯` (U+00AF) is the negative sign and part of the literal; `−` is the subtraction function. Forms: `12`, `¯3`, `1.5`, `.5` is refused, `1E6`, `1.5E¯3`. A literal with `.` or `E` is Float64; a literal with `¯` and no `.`/`E` is Int; otherwise Nat (D-10). ⟨`lex/numerals`, `lex/numeral-errors`⟩

**D-4 · Strands.** Two or more literals separated by spaces form one vector: `4 5 6 7`. A strand of mixed numeric kinds promotes to the join (D-11). AMENDED 2026-11-03: any adjacent array items strand (D-71); `a b` is the two-element vector. ⟨`parse/readme`, `parse/refusals`, `parse/nested`⟩

**D-5 · Character literals.** `'abc'` is a character vector; `'a'` is a character scalar; `''` is the empty character vector; `''''` is one quote. Elements are codepoints. ⟨`lex/strings-system`⟩

**D-6 · Nested arrays.** RULED 2026-10-27: v1 is flat; the README's `X ← 'sing' 'goddess' 'sing'` needs a boxed element kind, and v1 refuses it with `%nested-unsupported`. RULED 2026-11-03: the `%box` kind, scoped narrowly, over the rank-2 character matrix idiom, because `⍋X` on the README's `X` needs element-wise comparison the idiom cannot give; the idiom stays reachable as `⊃X`. D-70..D-77 are the rules. ⟨`atl/nested`, `parse/nested`⟩

**D-7 · Comments and separators.** `⍝` comments to end of line. `⋄` separates statements on one line. A newline ends a statement except inside a `∇` definition. ⟨`lex/names-glyphs`, `lex/strings-system`⟩

**D-8 · RULED and LANDED 2026-11-04 · ASCII input is bare words, in a session mode.** `)ASCII ON` turns the mode on, `)ASCII OFF` turns it off, and a bare `)ASCII` prints it; a session starts with it off. In the mode a line is rewritten before it is lexed, so the grammar never sees a word. A word of the table stands for its glyph, `_` opening a token before a digit stands for `¯`, and quoted text and everything after `⍝` (or `lamp`) is copied as typed. Every other word stays a name, so `iotas` is a name and `iota5` is a name, and a word stands alone only when spaces or punctuation part it from its neighbours. The table words are reserved while the mode is on, and a Unicode session keeps every name. A token and a refusal blame the column of the line as typed: `iota iota` refuses at column 6. A dfn's and a tradfn's body is stored as the glyph text it was rewritten to. `⍎` reads glyphs, not words. Output stays Unicode, and D-9's four ASCII spellings hold in both modes. The table gives one lowercase word per glyph: `upstile` ⌈, `downstile` ⌊, `rho` ⍴, `iota` ⍳, `times` ×, `divide` ÷, `domino` ⌹, `circle` ○, `circlestar` ⍟, `circlestile` ⌽, `circlebar` ⊖, `gradeup` ⍋, `gradedown` ⍒, `hydrant` ⍎, `thorn` ⍕, `transpose` ⍉, `equalunderbar` ≡, `equalunderbarslash` ≢, `commabar` ⍪, `epsilon` ∊, `epsilonunderbar` ⍷, `uparrow` ↑, `downarrow` ↓, `uptack` ⊥, `downtack` ⊤, `diaeresis` ¨, `lessorequal` ≤, `greaterorequal` ≥, `notequal` ≠, `logicalor` ∨, `logicaland` ∧, `logicalnor` ⍱, `logicalnand` ⍲, `lefttack` ⊣, `righttack` ⊢, `leftshoe` ⊂, `rightshoe` ⊃, `slashbar` ⌿, `backslashbar` ⍀, `jot` ∘, `diamond` ⋄, `leftarrow` ←, `del` ∇, `lamp` ⍝, `alpha` ⍺, `omega` ⍵, `alphaalpha` ⍺⍺, `omegaomega` ⍵⍵, `quadio` ⎕IO, `quadpp` ⎕PP, `quadtype` ⎕TYPE. The glyphs ASCII already spells (`+ - * | ~ , / \ . = < > ! ? ( ) [ ] { } ; : '`) need no word. Dyalog has no ASCII spelling of its glyphs, offering keyboard and prefix-key input instead; the 2024 tree's `ascii-map` (history b721975) is the ambiguous digraph scheme this replaces. AMENDED 2026-11-10: D-98 adds `downshoe` `upshoe` `zilde` and D-105 adds `tildediaeresis` `stardiaeresis` `jotdiaeresis`, 57 words in all. ⟨`atl/ascii`, `atl/readme-ascii`⟩

**D-9 · Four ASCII glyphs are canonical spellings, not transliterations.** `-` is `−`, `*` is `⋆`, `|` is `∣`, `~` is `∼`. ISO 13751 spells them in ASCII and every APL keyboard produces them; the lexer maps each to the inventory glyph and nothing else about D-8 changes. ⟨`lex/names-glyphs`⟩

## 3. Values

**D-10 · Four element kinds, one Bool convention.** `%nat` (Jock `@`), `%int` (Jock `Int`, ZigZag), `%real` (Float64), `%char` (codepoint). Booleans are `%nat` 0 and 1, as in APL; there is no separate Bool kind. ⟨`atl/vector-add`, `atl/residue-sign`, `atl/power-log`, `atl/display`⟩

**D-11 · The kind lattice is `%nat ⊂ %int ⊂ %real`; `%char` is apart.** A dyadic scalar function on two numeric kinds computes in the join. `%char` meets a numeric only at `=` and `≠`, and at `⍳` (index-of), `∊`, `⍋`, `⍒`. Anything else is a DOMAIN ERROR. AMENDED 2026-11-03: `%box` (D-70) is apart from the lattice too; a scalar function reaches into it (D-72) and meets the lattice at the leaves. AMENDED 2026-11-04: each kind has a width (D-15); the join of two kinds is computed at the session's width of the joined kind. RULED 2026-11-07: a comparison computes in the join too, so an integer meets a real as a real and `9007199254740993 ≠ 9007199254740992.0` is 0; GNU APL compares exactly and gives 1. ⟨`atl/residue-sign`, `atl/power-log`, `atl/errors`⟩

**D-12 · Result kinds are fixed per function, not inferred from values.** `÷`, `⍟`, `○`, `⋆` with a non-`%nat` exponent, and monadic `÷` answer `%real`. `⌊` and `⌈` on `%real` answer `%int`. Comparisons and the Booleans answer `%nat`. `−` on two `%nat` answers `%int` (never a crash on `3−5`). Everything else answers the join. This rule is what lets the inferencer type a statement without running it. ⟨`atl/power-log` (`2⋆10` → 1024), `atl/residue-sign` (`3−5` → ¯2), `atl/tradfn-div`⟩

**D-13 · An array is shape plus elements, row-major.** Shape is a list of naturals; rank is its length; a scalar has shape `~`. The abstract layout mirrors Lagoon's `$ray` field names (`shape`, `kind`, data) so that a packed erasure can replace the list-backed one without touching the primitives' contracts (plan P7). ⟨`atl/vector-add` (the layout is Lagoon's; plan section 1.1)⟩

**D-14 · `⎕IO` is 1 by default and may be set to 0 or 1.** It affects `⍳`, indexing, `⍋`, `⍒`, `⍳` (index-of), `∊`'s companion `⍸` (absent in v1), and `?`. ⟨`atl/index-origin` (assignment and the domain refusal)⟩

**D-15 · RULED and LANDED 2026-11-04 · `⎕TYPE` sets a storage width per kind.** The names are a kind word and a width in bits. Naturals take `UINT8` `UINT16` `UINT32` `UINT64` (Lagoon `%uint`, bloq 3 to 6), integers `INT8` `INT16` `INT32` `INT64` (`%int2`, bloq 3 to 6), and reals `REAL16` `REAL32` `REAL64` `REAL128` (`%i754`, bloq 4 to 7, the four float doors jojo jets). The setting is per kind and per session: `⎕TYPE←'REAL32'` sets the width of reals and leaves the other kinds alone, several names set several, and reading `⎕TYPE` gives all three, `UINT64 INT64 REAL64` by default, which is the v1 model. Any other value is a DOMAIN ERROR. Like `⎕IO` and `⎕PP`, `⎕TYPE` set inside a dfn is local to it (D-65). Every array a function or a numeral makes is made at the session's widths, with one exception: the shapes and indices `⍴B`, `⍳B`, `A⍳B`, `⍋B`, `⍒B` and `≢B` stay 64-bit naturals. Arrays read by name or by indexing keep the widths their elements have. Naturals and integers compute exactly, and a result that does not fit its width is a DOMAIN ERROR (RULED 2026-11-07, replacing the wrap ruled 2026-11-06): under `UINT8`, `200+100` refuses, and at the default width so do `18446744073709551615+1`, `¯9223372036854775808+¯1`, `-¯9223372036854775808`, `!21` and `⌊1E30`. A natural of 2^63 or more has no integer, so it refuses where a function needs one. GNU APL and APL2 promote an overflowing integer to a real instead; Atalante does not, because a change of kind is the program's to ask for. A real result that overflows to infinity is a DOMAIN ERROR too, as GNU APL's is. A real operation computes in the door of the real width, rounding once: under `REAL32`, `1÷3` is `0.33333334`, and under `REAL128` it carries 34 digits. An operand of another width converts to the session's real width first, exactly when widening and to nearest-even when narrowing. A strand or an indexed assignment that joins elements of one kind and different widths takes the wider width; catenation, disclose and every other function make their result at the session's width like any function. A numeral that does not fit the session's width of its kind is a DOMAIN ERROR: a natural of 2^w or more, an integer outside −2^(w−1)..2^(w−1)−1, a real that rounds to infinity. A numeral and a result follow one rule, so `⍳300` under `UINT8` refuses for the numeral, and `255+1` refuses for the result. A real numeral converts to its width correctly rounded, through hoon-138's `+grd`, at every width. Each real prints at its own width, so a `REAL32` value shows the shortest digits that round-trip at 32 bits, up to `⎕PP`. Characters stay codepoints and have no `⎕TYPE` name. ⟨`atl/types`, `atl/errors`⟩

### Nested arrays (P9, 2026-11-03)

**D-70 · The `%box` kind.** An element of an array may be any array: the fifth element kind, `%box`, holds one. A box has a shape like any array; its elements are arrays. Two normal forms keep every value unique: an array whose elements are all simple scalars of one class (numeric, or character) is simple, never a box; and a box scalar holding a simple scalar is that scalar (`⊂5` is `5`). A simple array of mixed characters and numbers, `1 'a' 2`, is a box of scalars. The Lagoon layout holds no box (roadmap P11): a box is the index ray of its shape over a Jock list of its elements in ravel order, and every structural function runs on that ray. ⟨`atl/nested`, `parse/nested`⟩

**D-71 · Strands (amends D-4).** Adjacent array items strand into a vector, one element per item: numerals, character literals, names, parenthesised expressions and indexed names. A numeral is one item, so `1 2 'a'` has three elements. A strand of numerals alone is D-4's simple vector; any other strand is the vector of its items' values, each enclosed, flattened by D-70 when every item is a simple scalar. A strand binds tighter than a function: `a b + c` is `(a b)+c`. An unbound name in a strand is a VALUE ERROR at its column. ⟨`parse/nested`, `atl/nested`, `parse/refusals` (an unbound name)⟩

**D-72 · Pervasion and permutation.** A scalar function (D-41) reaches into boxes: it applies element by element, D-25 at every level, and each level takes D-70's normal form; `1+(1 2)(3 4)` is `(2 3) (4 5)`. A structural function moves elements whole: `, ⌽ ⊖ ⍉ ⍴ ↑ ↓ / \` and bracket indexing permute a box as they permute a simple array, their left argument stays simple, and overtake and expand fill with the prototype (D-77). `A⍳B` and `A∊B` compare elements by match (D-73). `A,B` with a box on either side, or characters on one side and numbers on the other, catenates the elements into a box, flattened by D-70. `f/` and `f\` over a box vector fold its elements, each result enclosed; over a box of higher rank they are held back, as are `f.g` and `∘.g` over any box. ⟨`atl/nested`⟩

**D-73 · Enclose, disclose, first, pick, depth, match, enlist.** `⊂B` encloses: a simple scalar is its own enclosure, anything else becomes a box scalar. `⊃B` discloses a box whose elements are simple scalars or vectors of one class into the simple array of shape `(⍴B),L`, `L` the longest element, each padded with its fill; on a simple array it is the identity. `↑B` is the first element, disclosed (`↑'ab' 'cd'` is `'ab'`), and the prototype of an empty array. `A⊃B` picks: `A` a scalar index in `⎕IO`, or a vector of indices followed through nested boxes; the element, disclosed. `≡B` is depth: 0 for a simple scalar, 1 for a simple array, one more than the deepest element for a box. `A≡B` is match: 1 when the shapes agree and every element matches (numbers match across kinds, a character never matches a number, a box never matches a simple array), else 0. `∊B` enlists: the vector of every leaf in ravel order, a box of scalars when the leaves mix classes. Held back: `A⊂B` partition, `⊃` of a box whose elements are boxes or of rank 2 or more, `≢`. ⟨`atl/nested`⟩

**D-74 · Each (amends D-43).** `f¨B` applies `f` to each element of `B` and encloses each result; the result has `B`'s shape and takes D-70's normal form, so `⍳¨1 2 3` is `(,1) (1 2) (1 2 3)` and `{⍵×2}¨1 2 3` is still `2 4 6`. `A f¨B` pairs elements under D-25. Over a simple array a scalar function under each is the scalar map, and the v1 vectors hold. ⟨`atl/nested`, `atl/dfns`⟩

**D-75 · Order.** `⍋` and `⍒` on a box vector sort by a total order on arrays: a simple array before a box; among simple arrays, numbers before characters, then the ravels in lexicographic order with a proper prefix first, then the shapes; among boxes, the elements in lexicographic order by this same order, then the shapes. The sort is stable (D-42). The README's `⍋X` is `2 4 6 1 3 5`. ⟨`atl/nested`⟩

**D-76 · Display of a box (amends D-45).** A box prints as the strand that builds it, so its display is ATL source: a numeric scalar bare, a character scalar or vector in quotes with a quote doubled, a numeric vector in parentheses (`(1 2)`; `(,5)` for one element; `(⍳0)` for none), a box element in parentheses as a strand, a simple array of rank 2 or more as `(shape⍴ravel)`; one space between elements. A box scalar prints as `⊂` before its element. A box of rank 2 prints one row per line, a higher rank its planes separated by a blank line, with no column alignment. `X[⍋X]` prints `'goddess' 'of' 'rage' 'sing' 'sing' 'the'`, as the 2024 README did. AMENDED 2026-11-09: this is the default; `)DISPLAY APL2` prints APL2's layout instead (D-99). ⟨`atl/nested`, `atl/readme-red`, `atl/display-apl2`⟩

**D-77 · Fill.** The prototype of an array is its first element with every leaf replaced by that leaf's fill, `0` for a number and a space for a character; the prototype of an empty array is `0`. Overtake, expand and `↑` of an empty array supply it. AMENDED 2026-11-07: an empty array made from another keeps that array's prototype, so `↑0⍴⊂1 2 3` is `0 0 0` and `2↑0⍴⊂1 2 3` is `(0 0 0) (0 0 0)`; an empty box holds its prototype as its only stored item, which no index names. Two empty boxes match when their shapes and prototypes match. ⟨`atl/nested`, `atl/gnu-order`⟩

### Nesting, the rest (P9 closed, 2026-11-03)

**D-78 · Partition.** `A⊂B`: `A` a vector of non-negative integers as long as `B` (a scalar extends), `B` a vector. An element of the result starts where `A` exceeds the count before it; a 0 drops `B`'s element. `1 1 2 2⊂'abcd'` is `'ab' 'cd'`; `1 0 1⊂'abc'` is `'a' 'c'`. AMENDED 2026-11-04: past rank 1 the counts partition every line along the last axis, or along axis `k` in `A⊂[k]B`. The result has `B`'s shape with that axis replaced by the number of groups, and each element is a vector: `1 1 2⊂2 3⍴⍳6` is `(1 2) (,3)` over `(4 5) (,6)`. ⟨`atl/partition`⟩

**D-79 · Tally and not-match.** `≢B` is the length of `B`'s first axis, 1 for a scalar. `A≢B` is `∼A≡B`. ⟨`atl/tally`⟩

**D-80 · Disclose, in full (amends D-73).** `⊃B` takes elements that are scalars or all of one rank `R`; the result has shape `(⍴B),S` with `S` the per-axis maximum, each element padded at the end of each axis with its fill, a scalar placed at the origin of its cell; nested elements make a box, padded with the prototype (D-77). ⟨`atl/disclose`⟩

**D-81 · Boxes under the operators (amends D-72).** Along any axis the lines of a box fold under `f/` and `f\` as D-72 folds a vector. `∘.g` takes every pair of elements, `f.g` every row of `A` against every column of `B`, elements whole; the results take D-70's normal form. ⟨`atl/box-operators`⟩

### Axis specification and the held-back primitives (P10, 2026-11-03)

**D-82 · Axis specification `[k]`.** A numeral in `⎕IO`, in brackets after `⌽ ⊖ , / \ ⌿ ⍀` and after a derived `f/ f\ f⌿ f⍀`: `⌽[k]B` reverses along axis `k`, `A⌽[k]B` rotates, `A,[k]B` catenates, `f/[k]B` reduces, `f\[k]B` scans, `A/[k]B` replicates, `A\[k]B` expands. `k` is integral and within the rank, else an AXIS ERROR; a fractional `k` after `,` laminates (D-92, AMENDED 2026-11-04). AMENDED 2026-11-07, after GNU APL: an axis written as numerals that name no axis is an AXIS ERROR when the line parses, as are a numeral list after a function other than a scalar one (D-96), a character, and a fraction that sits between no two axes (`⌽[1 2]M`, `M+['a']M`, `,[1.5E1]A`). An axis that is an expression, such as `,[⍳0]A`, is a SYNTAX ERROR, and so is a second axis on one function, `+/[1][2]M`. ⟨`atl/axis`, `atl/gnu-order`, `parse/axis`, `parse/refusals`⟩

**D-83 · Past rank 2 (amends D-42, D-43).** Reduction, scan, replicate, expand, reverse and rotate work along the last axis, the first with `⌿ ⍀ ⊖`, or the named one, of an array of any rank. `A⌽B` takes a scalar or one count per line, an array of the shape of `B` without that axis. `A↑B` and `A↓B` take one count per leading axis of `B`; fewer counts than axes leave the trailing axes whole; a scalar `B` is treated as having that many axes of length 1. ⟨`atl/rank3`, `atl/take-drop-rotate`⟩

**D-84 · Table and first-axis catenate.** `⍪B` is `B` as a matrix: a scalar 1×1, a vector n×1, a matrix itself, a higher rank its first axis by the rest. `A⍪B` catenates on the first axis; a vector against a matrix is one row, two vectors are two rows, a scalar extends. ⟨`atl/table`⟩

**D-85 · Dyadic transpose.** `A⍉B`: `A` a vector of axes in `⎕IO`, one per axis of `B`, naming every result axis at least once; result axis `j` has the length of the shortest axis of `B` that `A` maps to it, and a repeated axis takes the diagonal. `(⍳⍴⍴B)⍉B` is `B`, `2 1⍉M` is `⍉M`, `1 1⍉M` is the diagonal. ⟨`atl/transpose`⟩

**D-86 · Decode and encode on integers and reals (amends D-42).** `A⊥B` evaluates the polynomial in the join kind of `A` and `B`, a scalar radix extending, `B` a scalar or vector. `A⊤B` encodes with floor and residue (APL2's rule), so negative and real values encode as they do there: `2 2 2⊤¯1` is `1 1 1`, `10 10⊤1.5` is `0 1.5`; `B` a scalar gives a vector, a vector gives a `(⍴A),(⍴B)` matrix; a 0 radix takes what remains. ⟨`atl/encode-decode`⟩

**D-87 · Find.** `A⍷B` is a Boolean of `B`'s shape, 1 where a copy of `A` begins, elements compared by match (D-73); `A` of rank at most that of `B`, any rank (AMENDED 2026-11-04, from rank 2), with `A`'s shape padded by leading 1s; a scalar `A` is `A=B` by match. ⟨`atl/find`⟩

**D-88 · Format** (AMENDED 2026-11-09 by D-102)**.** `⍕B` is `B`'s display (D-45, D-76, under `)DISPLAY APL2` D-99) as a character array: a vector for rank at most 1 and for a box of rank at most 1, a matrix for rank 2, one row per line of the display; past rank 2 (AMENDED 2026-11-04) an array of `B`'s leading axes by the text width, its rows the display's rows without the blank lines; a character array is itself. `D⍕B` prints each number with `D` decimals in fixed point, columns right-aligned to the widest; `W D⍕B` gives each column width `W`, and a number that does not fit is a DOMAIN ERROR. Negatives carry `¯`. ⟨`atl/format`⟩

**D-89 · Execute.** `⍎B` runs the character vector `B` as a line in the current environment, through the compiler, and is the line's value; its assignments persist. A line without a value (a system command, a `∇` header, nothing) is a VALUE ERROR (AMENDED 2026-11-07, after GNU APL and APL2; it was a DOMAIN ERROR); a refusal in the line is the refusal. ⟨`atl/execute`, `atl/gnu-order`⟩

**D-90 · Matrix divide.** `⌹B` inverts a square matrix. `A⌹B` solves `B X = A` for square `B` and gives the least-squares solution when `B` has more rows than columns (through the normal equations); `A` a vector is one column, a matrix several. Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting over reals; the result is `%real`; a singular `B` is a DOMAIN ERROR. AMENDED 2026-11-07: a scalar is a 1×1 matrix, so `⌹4` is `0.25` and `⌹0` is a DOMAIN ERROR. ⟨`atl/solve`, `atl/gnu-order`⟩

**D-91 · Dfn operands (amends D-63).** The operand of `.` and `∘.` may be a dfn: `∘.{⍺×⍵}`, `+.{⍺×⍵}`. ⟨`atl/dfn-operands`, `parse/operators`⟩

**D-96 · Axis on a scalar function (2026-11-06).** `A f[K] B` with `f` a scalar dyadic function: `K` names, in `⎕IO`, the axes of the higher-rank argument that the axes of the lower-rank one run along, one distinct axis each, so the lower is spread to the higher's shape and cell `i` of the higher meets the lower's cell `i[K]`. `(3 5⍴1)+[1]⍳3` adds 1, 2 and 3 to the three rows; `(3 5⍴1)+[2]⍳5` adds `⍳5` to every row; `K` may be a list, `(4 3 5⍴1)+[1 2] 4 3⍴⍳3`. A `K` of another length than the lower argument's rank, or naming an axis twice or past the rank, is an AXIS ERROR; named axes whose lengths are not the lower argument's shape are a LENGTH ERROR. Only a scalar function's axis may be a list; any other function's axis is one numeral (D-82). From GNU APL's testcases (APL2). ⟨`gnu/Add`, `parse/axis`⟩

**D-97 · `⍬` and n-wise reduction (2026-11-09).** `⍬` is the empty numeric vector, `0⍴0`, written as one array item: `1 ⍬ 2` is a three-element strand and `⍬ ⍬` a two-element one. `A f/[k] B` and `A f⌿ B` reduce each window of `|A|` consecutive elements along axis `k` (the last for `/`, the first for `⌿`), so the axis becomes `1+n−|A|` long for an axis of length `n`: `3+/1 2 3 4 5 6` is `6 9 12 15`. A negative `A` reverses each window before reducing, so `¯2-/1 4 9` is `3 5`; `A` of 0 gives `1+n` identities of `f`, and a scalar `B` is a one-element vector, so `0+/99` is `0 0`. `A` is one whole number: a fraction or a character is a DOMAIN ERROR, more than one number a LENGTH ERROR, a matrix a RANK ERROR, and `|A|` past `n+1` a DOMAIN ERROR, as in GNU APL. ⟨`atl/nwise`, `atl/sets`, `parse/nested`, `gnu/Reduce_N_wise`, `gnu/Zilde`⟩

**D-98 · The set functions `∪ ∩ ∼` (2026-11-09).** `∪B` is `B`'s elements at their first occurrence: `∪'mississippi'` is `'misp'`. `A∼B` is `A` without the elements of `B`, `A∩B` the elements of `A` that are in `B`, and `A∪B` is `∪A,B`, GNU APL's symmetric union, so duplicates leave both sides: `1 1 2 2∪1 1 3 3 5 5` is `1 2 3 5`. Dyalog defines `A∪B` as `A,B∼A`, which keeps `A`'s duplicates; neither IBM nor ISO defines it. Elements compare by match (D-73), so a box is an element: `'GO' 'TO' 'IT'∼'GOTO' 'IT'` is `'GO' 'TO'`. Each result keeps the left argument's order, and an empty one keeps its prototype (D-77). Both arguments of `∩` and `∪`, the left of `∼` and the argument of monadic `∪` are scalars or vectors, else a RANK ERROR; a scalar is a one-element vector. Under `)ASCII ON` the words are `downshoe` `upshoe` and `zilde` (D-8). ⟨`atl/sets`, `parse/nested`, `gnu/Union`, `gnu/Intersection`, `gnu/Without`⟩

**D-99 · `)DISPLAY` and APL2's layout (2026-11-09).** `)DISPLAY APL2` prints a box in IBM APL2's layout, as GNU APL prints it; `)DISPLAY STRAND` restores the strand (D-76), the default; `)DISPLAY` alone names the current setting, and any other argument is a SYNTAX ERROR. The setting covers the session's printing and `⍕` (D-88), and a simple array prints as D-45 has it under both. In APL2's layout each item is a block of lines. Adjacent columns are parted by the larger of the two columns' spacings, where an item's spacing is its rank, plus 1 unless it is simple character data (APL2 Language Reference p. 138): `'A' 'B'` abut, `'TO' 'KEN'` prints ` TO KEN `, and `(2 6) (3 4)` prints ` 2 6  3 4 `. A box with any item other than a simple scalar takes one blank on each side, so `⊂'HIM' 'HIS'` prints `  HIM HIS  `. An empty item prints as one blank and is spaced as a scalar, as in GNU APL: `1 ⍬ 2` prints ` 1   2 `. In a column, simple numbers align right and other items left; rows are parted by a blank line when an item has rank 2 or more, and planes as in D-45. ⟨`atl/display-apl2`, `gnu/*`⟩

**D-104 · Roll, deal and `⎕RL` (2026-11-09).** `?B` rolls each element `n` of `B` to an integer drawn uniformly from `⎕IO` to `n−1+⎕IO`; the result has `B`'s shape. `A?B` deals `A` distinct integers from `⍳B` in random order. `B`'s elements and `A` are whole numbers, positive for `?`: anything else is a DOMAIN ERROR, as is `A` past `B`, and `A?B` with more than one number on either side is a LENGTH ERROR. The draws come from one stream per session, `/lib/rand`'s Philox4x32-10, which is NOT cryptographic. The host's entropy (jojo's `eny`) seeds it at the session's first line. `⎕RL←n`, for a natural below 2^64, reseeds it, so the draws after it repeat; `⎕RL` reads the seed. With no host, as in the corpus, the seed is 0. GNU APL has its own generator, so the same `⎕RL` gives other numbers there. ⟨`atl/roll-deal`, `gnu/Roll`, `gnu/Deal`⟩

**D-100 · `∨` and `∧` as GCD and LCM (2026-11-09).** `A∨B` is the greatest common divisor and `A∧B` the least common multiple, so on 0 and 1 they are still the Boolean or and and: `30∨36` is 6 and `30∧36` is 180. The divisor is never negative, and the multiple takes the sign of `A×B`: `¯4∧6` is ¯12. Integers divide exactly. A real divides as its shortest decimal, the number as it prints, so `1E30∨1E10` is `1E10` and `3∨3.6` is 0.6. Euclid on the binary value would give powers of two, since 1E30 is not 10^30 in binary; APL2 and GNU APL reach the same answers through the comparison tolerance, which Atalante does not have (D-11). A multiple past its width is a DOMAIN ERROR (D-15): naturals hold 64 bits, so `2028259601∧9094863431` is 18446744073709551031 where GNU APL promotes it to a real. `∨/` starts from 0 and `∧/` from 1. ⟨`atl/next-tier`, `gnu/And`, `gnu/Or`⟩

**D-101 · The hyperbolic circle codes (2026-11-09).** `5○B`, `6○B` and `7○B` are sinh, cosh and tanh; `¯5○B`, `¯6○B` and `¯7○B` are their inverses. They are computed through e^x−1 and ln(1+x) in Kahan's forms, so a small argument keeps its digits: `5○1E¯10` is `1E¯10`. `¯6○B` takes `B ≥ 1` and `¯7○B` takes `|B| < 1`; outside those, and for the complex codes 8 to 12, the result is a DOMAIN ERROR. ⟨`atl/next-tier`, `gnu/Circle_Functions`⟩

**D-102 · `⍕`'s exact digits and its column pairs (amends D-88, 2026-11-09).** `⍕` with a left argument rounds the exact binary value of a real, half to even, as C's printf does: `5 1⍕0.15` is `0.1`, since 0.15 is 0.1499999999999999944…, and `30 20⍕1÷3` prints its true 20 decimals. The left argument is `D`, `W D`, or one `W D` pair per column. A negative `D` asks for exponent form with `|D|` significant digits: `12 ¯4⍕9.9996` is `1.000E1`. An exponent column pads on the right to its widest cell, so its mantissas line up. `W` 0 is the column's natural width, one blank after the column before it. Any other length is a LENGTH ERROR, and a negative `W` is a DOMAIN ERROR. ⟨`atl/next-tier`, `atl/format`, `gnu/Format_by_spec`⟩

**D-103 · Negative replicate, and pick through a box path (2026-11-09).** In `A/B` and `A⌿B` a negative count `−n` skips its element of `B` and puts `n` fills in its place, as in APL2: `1 ¯2 1/'abc'` is `'a  c'`. Expand keeps its non-negative counts. In `A⊃B` with a box `A`, each item of `A` picks at one depth, either a scalar from a vector or a vector of one index per axis from an array of any rank: `(⊂2 2)⊃M` is `M[2;2]`. An index of the wrong length is a RANK ERROR, and one out of range is an INDEX ERROR. ⟨`atl/next-tier`, `gnu/Replicate`, `gnu/Pick`⟩

**D-105 · The operators `⍨`, `⍣` and `⍤` (2026-11-09).** These take a function on the left; an array there is a SYNTAX ERROR, and so is an array-less `⍤`. A parenthesised derived function is one function, so `2 (+⍣40) 5` is 85 and `(∘.≤)⍨1 2 3` applies the outer product.
- `f⍨B` is `B f B`, and `A f⍨B` is `B f A`: `2-⍨5` is 3.
- `f⍣n` applies `f` `n` times, `n` a whole number (natural, integer or whole real), and `A f⍣n B` applies `A f` each time: `{⍵×2}⍣3 1` is 8. A negative `n` would need an inverse, so it is UNIMPLEMENTED; any other `n` is a DOMAIN ERROR.
- `f⍣g` applies `f` until `(f x) g x` is 1, so `{1+÷⍵}⍣= 1` is the golden ratio. `g` must give one Boolean, else a DOMAIN ERROR. There is no limit on the number of steps: a `g` that never gives 1 runs until interrupted, as in APL2 and GNU APL.
- `f⍤k` applies `f` to the rank-`k` cells of its argument and mixes the results as `⊃` does (D-80), padding them to one shape. `k` is `c`, `(l r)` or `(c l r)`, one to three whole numbers of kind natural or integer: `c` is for the monadic call, and `l` and `r` for the left and right arguments; with `(l r)` the monadic call takes `r` (Dyalog's `⌽3⍴⌽k`). Any other `k` is a DOMAIN ERROR. A rank past the argument's is the argument's, and a negative `k` counts down from the argument's rank. Cells of no elements still number one per frame position; with no cells at all, `f` runs once on a prototype cell for the shape and kind of the empty result, so `{⍴⍵}⍤1⊢2 0⍴0` is `2 1⍴0`.
- In the dyadic case the two frames must agree, or one of them must be empty, which repeats its one cell. Frames of different rank are a RANK ERROR, and frames that disagree otherwise are a LENGTH ERROR.

Under `)ASCII ON` the words are `tildediaeresis`, `stardiaeresis` and `jotdiaeresis` (D-8). A right operand that is an array is one numeral, a name or a parenthesised expression (D-95), so two ranks are written `+⍤(0 1)`. ⟨`atl/operators`, `gnu/Commute`, `gnu/OperPower`, `gnu/Rank`⟩

### The rest of §9 (2026-11-04)

**D-92 · Laminate: a fractional axis after `,`.** `A,[k]B` with `k` a numeral that is not an integer joins `A` and `B` along a new axis of length 2, placed between axes `⌊k` and `⌈k` (in `⎕IO`). `A` and `B` have one shape, and a scalar extends to the other's. `1 2 3,[0.5]4 5 6` is a 2×3 matrix and `1 2 3,[1.5]4 5 6` a 3×2 one. With `⎕IO←0` the first position is `[¯0.5]`. Monadic `,[k]B` gives `B` a new axis of length 1 there. Shapes that differ are a LENGTH ERROR and ranks that differ a RANK ERROR. A fractional axis outside the rank, or after any function but `,`, is an AXIS ERROR. ⟨`atl/laminate`, `parse/dfn-extras`⟩

**D-93 · A default left argument, `⍺←`.** In a dfn body, `⍺←X` binds `⍺` to `X` when the dfn was called monadically and does nothing when it was called with a left argument, so `{⍺←10 ⋄ ⍺+⍵}` adds 10 to a lone argument. Any other assignment to `⍺` or `⍵` is an ordinary local assignment (D-65). ⟨`atl/dfn-defaults`⟩

**D-94 · Error guards, `codes::expr`.** A dfn statement `codes::expr` arms a guard. If a later statement of the same call fails with an error whose number is in `codes`, or 0 is in `codes`, the call's result is `expr`. The expression runs in the environment the guard was armed in. A later guard replaces an earlier one. The numbers follow Dyalog where the error class is shared: SYNTAX 2, INDEX 3, RANK 4, LENGTH 5, VALUE 6, DOMAIN 11, and NONCE 16 for an unimplemented case. VALENCE folds into 2, since Dyalog reports valence as syntax, and AXIS takes 9, which Dyalog leaves unused. An emitter mismatch is never caught. A parse-time error on a later body line is caught. One on the guard's own line is not, because a body line parses whole before it runs. ⟨`atl/error-guards`, `parse/dfn-extras`⟩

**D-95 · Dfn operators.** A dfn whose body names `⍺⍺`, outside any nested dfn, is a monadic operator; one naming `⍵⍵` is a dyadic operator. `⍺⍺` and `⍵⍵` lex as single names. The operator takes the function to its left as `⍺⍺` and, when dyadic, the function to its right as `⍵⍵`, and the derived function is called as any dfn is. `+{⍺⍺/⍵}1 2 3` is 6. `OP←{⍺⍺/⍵}` names a monadic operator, which the signature classes as 6 (7 for a dyadic one), so `+OP 1 2 3` parses as `(+ OP)(1 2 3)`. An operand is a function, a glyph, a function name or a dfn literal, or an array (AMENDED 2026-11-06). An array directly left of an operator is its left operand, so `2 {⍺⍺+⍵} 3` is 5. The right operand of a dyadic operator is the function to its right, or else an array that is one numeral or character literal, an array name, or a parenthesised expression: `+{⍺⍺/⍵⍵↑⍵}2 ⍳5` is 3. An array operand is evaluated where the operator is applied and bound in the body as an array. A function operand is resolved there too, `⍺⍺` and `⍵⍵` to the enclosing operator's own operands and a local dfn's name to that dfn, and it runs in the frames around the operator, not inside it. `∇∇` names the operator itself, of the operator's valence, so `⍺⍺ ∇∇ ⍵-1` recurs; inside an operator `∇` is the derived function, the same operands again. A derived function with an array operand applies to the array on its right; applying it dyadically through parentheses, `1 (2 OP) 3`, is held back. ⟨`atl/dfn-operators`, `parse/dfn-extras`, `lex/dfns`⟩

## 4. Syntax and evaluation

**D-20 · Right-to-left; a function takes everything to its right.** `2×3+4` is 14. A dyadic function's left argument is the single array immediately to its left. Parentheses group. ⟨`parse/readme`, `parse/operators`⟩

**D-21 · Operators bind before functions and take their operand from the left.** `+/`, `+\`, `+.×`, `∘.×`, `f¨`. The derived function then follows D-20. Operands are primitive functions, user-defined functions and dfns (D-63: `{⍺+⍵}/1 2 3`); no derived-function operands (`+/¨` is on the roadmap). ⟨`parse/readme`, `parse/operators`, `parse/dfns`⟩

**D-22 · Monadic or dyadic is decided by what stands to the left.** A function with an array (or a parenthesised expression, or a name bound to an array) on its left is dyadic; otherwise monadic. A name's valence is looked up in the store at compile time (D-52); a dfn, and a name bound to one, is ambivalent (D-64, D-67). ⟨`parse/readme`, `parse/operators`, `parse/refusals`, `parse/dfns`⟩

**D-23 · Bracket indexing.** `X[i]`, `M[i;j]`, `M[i;]`, `M[;j]`. Indices are arrays; the result shape is the catenation of the index shapes. An index outside `⎕IO`-based bounds is an INDEX ERROR. AMENDED 2026-11-07, after GNU APL: the checks run in a fixed order, the number of indices against the rank first (RANK ERROR), then each index's domain, then its range. An integer of either sign or a whole real is a position, so `V[¯1]` and `V[1E30]` are INDEX ERRORs; a fraction, a character or a box is a DOMAIN ERROR, so `5[0.5]` is a RANK ERROR and `V[0.5]` a DOMAIN ERROR. Indexed assignment `X[i] ← v` is v1. AMENDED 2026-11-03: on a box the elements move whole (D-72), so `X[⍋X]` on the README's `X` is the sorted strand; an indexed assignment whose value or target is a box, or that puts characters among numbers, leaves a box. ⟨`parse/indexing` (parsing only), `atl/nested`⟩

**D-24 · Assignment `←` is an expression whose value is its right side.** At the top level a statement whose last act is an assignment prints nothing. Variables are global unless localised in a `∇` header (D-31). ⟨`parse/readme`, `parse/statements`, `atl/indexing` (indexed assignment), `atl/readme-red`⟩

**D-25 · Scalar extension.** A scalar or one-element vector extends to the other argument's shape. Otherwise shapes must match exactly; a rank mismatch is a RANK ERROR, a length mismatch a LENGTH ERROR. AMENDED 2026-11-03: a box scalar extends like any scalar, and the rule applies again inside each pair of elements (D-72). ⟨`atl/vector-add`, `atl/errors`, `atl/nested`⟩

## 5. Functions

**D-30 · Tradfns.** `∇ R←A F B` opens a definition; a lone `∇` closes it. Headers: `∇F`, `∇F B`, `∇A F B`, each with optional `R←`. Lines between are statements. The REPL reads the body until the closing `∇` (D-62). ⟨`parse/readme`, `parse/statements`, `parse/refusals`, `atl/tradfn-div`, `atl/caesar`, `atl/readme-red`⟩

**D-31 · Locals are declared in the header after `;`.** `∇ R←A F B;T;U` makes `T` and `U` local; everything else is global (dynamic scope, as in classical APL). Arguments and the result are local. ⟨`parse/statements`, `atl/caesar` (locals restored: `A` unbound after the call), `atl/readme-red`⟩

**D-32 · RULED 2026-10-28 · Control flow is dfns with guards, in v2.** v1 has none: a tradfn body is straight-line. v2 adds dfns `{⍺ ⍵}` with guards `cond: expr`, the modern form; `→` and labels, Budd's classical form, are omitted for good. Guards compose with the emitter's straight-line formulas where `→` would need a label table and a loop in the generated Nock. Both were expressible on the v1 AST; dfns are what enters. LANDED 2026-11-03 as P8: D-63..D-69.

**D-33 · A user function's valence is fixed by its header and checked at the call.** Calling a dyadic function monadically is a VALENCE ERROR. A dfn has no header: its valence is settled at each call (D-67). ⟨`parse/refusals`⟩

**D-34 · A tradfn body is compiled on first call, not at the closing `∇`.** The definition stores the header and the body as token lines. The first call parses and emits the body against the store's signature at that moment and caches the formula keyed by that signature; a later change of class for any name the body reads (array to function, a new valence, unbound to bound) invalidates the cache and the next call recompiles. A body may therefore name a function defined after it, as in classical APL, and every executed line is still compiled Nock. A name unbound at the time of the call is a VALUE ERROR at that call. A dfn body is compiled the same way (D-67). ⟨`atl/readme-green` (bodies captured unparsed), `atl/tradfn-div`, `atl/caesar` (compiled on call), `atl/tradfn-cache` (the hit, the class-change re-parse, the redefinition; the cache holds the formulas, and every body statement runs through its formula against the tree evaluation)⟩

### Dfns (P8, 2026-11-03)

**D-63 · Dfns.** `{…}` is a function value: the body between the braces is statements separated by `⋄` or by line ends (D-69). `⍵` names the right argument and `⍺` the left; `∇` inside the body names the dfn itself. A dfn stands wherever a function stands: applied to the array on its right, with an array on its left, and as the operand of `/ \ ⌿ ⍀ ¨` (`{⍺+⍵}/1 2 3`). Dfns nest. AMENDED 2026-11-04: dfn operators (D-95), a default left argument (D-93) and error guards (D-94) have landed, as has a dfn as the operand of `.` or `∘.` (D-91). ⟨`lex/dfns`, `parse/dfns`, `atl/dfns`⟩

**D-64 · A name takes a dfn by assignment and is then an ambivalent function.** `F←{⍺÷⍵}` binds `F` with class ambivalent (D-22, D-52): `F 6` is monadic, `5 F 6` dyadic. Only a brace literal is assigned as a function; `F←+` is a SYNTAX ERROR. The name is one name: an array assigned to it later makes it an array (`DIV←3`), a `∇` definition replaces the dfn, and a dfn assignment replaces a tradfn. `)FNS` lists it and `)ERASE` erases it. ⟨`parse/dfns`, `atl/dfns`⟩

**D-65 · Dfn scope is lexical.** A call opens a frame. An assignment in the body binds a local of that call, never a global; a read falls through the frame, then the frames of the enclosing dfns, then the store. `⍺` and `⍵` belong to the innermost dfn alone. A global dfn runs over the store, not over its caller's locals: with `a←1`, `K←{a+⍵}` and `L←{a←100 ⋄ K ⍵}`, `L 1` is 2. `⎕IO` and `⎕PP` assigned in a body are local to the call. A tradfn keeps D-31's dynamic scope and sees no dfn frame. ⟨`atl/dfns` (`H 5` then `a`; the nested read; `L 1`; `⎕IO`)⟩

**D-66 · The result is the first statement that is not an assignment; a guard chooses.** Statements run in order. An assignment, of an array or of a dfn, continues. A guard `cond: expr` evaluates `cond`, which must be a scalar 0 or 1 (DOMAIN ERROR otherwise); at 1 the value of `expr` is the result, at 0 the next statement runs. Any other statement's value is the result. A body that runs off its end has no result: VALUE ERROR. A guard outside a dfn is a SYNTAX ERROR. ⟨`parse/dfns`, `atl/dfns`, `atl/dfn-lines`⟩

**D-67 · A dfn is ambivalent; valence is settled at the call, and the body is compiled on first call (D-34).** `⍺` in a body called monadically is a VALUE ERROR at that call. The body is kept as source lines and parsed, emitted and run on the call: each line against the signature as it stands, each statement through its formula against the tree evaluation. The entry signature carries `⍺` only when bound, so monadic and dyadic calls compile apart. A named dfn caches the lines a call reached, keyed by the entry signature; a later call that goes past a false guard parses the lines beyond and extends the cache. Guards and the statement order belong to the body runner, as a tradfn's line order does: a guard's condition and its expression are each a formula, the branch between them is not Nock. ⟨`atl/dfns` (`{⍺} 3`), `atl/dfn-lines` (`SIGN`: the cache extended past its guards)⟩

**D-68 · Class changes inside a line thread the signature.** Each `⋄`-separated statement parses against the signature the statements before it leave: an assignment binds an array, a dfn assignment an ambivalent function, a header its valence. `G←{⍵+1} ⋄ G 3` is 4 with `G` an array before the line. Inside a body each line parses against the signature the lines before it left at run time, the same rule with values in hand. ⟨`parse/dfns` (`G←{⍵} ⋄ G 3`), `atl/dfns`⟩

## 7. Compilation model

**D-50 · A statement compiles to a Nock formula over the subject `[rt env]`.** `rt` is the runtime core: the primitives as Jock function values. `env` is the session environment: the variable store, the system variables, and the user function definitions. The formula's product is the evaluation result, value and environment or a refusal. The REPL evaluates it with `eval`, prints the value per D-45, and keeps the environment. AMENDED 2026-10-27 before P5 landed: the product carries a refusal too, so the formula needs no branch; the formula is emitted as Nockasm and lowered by the reference expander. ⟨every red-prompt case, through the session differential; `atl/nock`⟩

**D-51 · Primitives are called by axis, never by name, in emitted Nock.** The emitter knows the runtime core's layout; a primitive call is `9 2 10 [6 args] 0 axis` against the subject (verified 2026-10-27 against Jock `eval`). Changing the runtime core's layout re-blesses every emission vector. ⟨`atl/nock` (`%call 2 (%edit 6 … .g-x)` by name), `atl/goldens` (the lowered nouns), the P5 probe of 2026-10-27⟩

**D-52 · Names resolve at compile time against the store's shape, not its values.** A name's kind (array or function, valence) decides parsing (D-22), and the statements of one line thread it (D-68); its value is read at run time. An unbound name in a top-level statement is a VALUE ERROR at compile time; AMENDED 2026-11-04 (S-24), a name assigned further right in the same statement is bound where it is read, since evaluation runs right to left, and the refusal blames the rightmost unbound read, the first one evaluation reaches: `(~R ∊ R ∘.× R) / R ← 1↓⍳R` with `R` unbound refuses at column 25; inside a tradfn body the check is deferred to the first call (D-34). ⟨`parse/refusals`, `atl/unbound-column`⟩

**D-53 · Inference is an optimisation, never a semantics.** Budd's rank, shape and kind propagation chooses a specialised primitive (`%add-real-vector`) over the generic one. A program's value does not depend on whether inference ran. The differential vector `generic == specialised` pins this on every example. ⟨the evaluation corpus, every red statement both ways; `atl/goldens` (`+/n` chooses `g-reduce`, `n[2]` chooses `g-index1`)⟩

**D-54 · The green prompt shows, the red prompt runs.** `🍏>` prints the AST (and with `:nock`, the emitted formula) without evaluating; `🍎>` evaluates. Both are the same compiler up to the last stage. ⟨`atl/readme-green` (the tree), `atl/nock` (`:nock`, the Nockasm program), `atl/goldens` (`:noun`), `atl/commands` (the modes)⟩

## 8. The session

**D-60 · System variables.** `⎕IO` (D-14), `⎕PP` (D-45), `⎕TYPE` (D-15, the widths). Reading one is an expression; assigning one is validated (`⎕IO ← 2` is a DOMAIN ERROR). ⟨`lex/strings-system` (lexing only), `atl/index-origin`⟩

**D-61 · System commands.** `)GREEN`, `)RED` and `)TOGGLE` choose the prompt mode (D-54) for the lines that follow (added 2026-10-27 for the REPL). A `)` that opens a line and is followed by a name is a command token; elsewhere `)` closes a parenthesis. `)VARS` lists variables, `)FNS` lists functions, `)ERASE name`, `)OFF` ends the session. `)ASCII ON` and `)ASCII OFF` (added 2026-11-04) turn D-8's ASCII words on and off. `)PATH BOTH`, `)PATH INTERP` and `)PATH EMIT` (added 2026-11-04, P11b) choose how the red prompt evaluates. BOTH, the default, runs the interpreter and the emitted formula and compares them (D-53). INTERP runs the interpreter alone, and EMIT the emitted formula alone, which is the fast path. A bare `)PATH` prints the current choice. ⟨`atl/path`⟩ `)SAVE` is the kernel's checkpoint and needs no command; it is parsed and answers that. ⟨`lex/strings-system`, `atl/commands`, `kernel/sieve` (`)VARS` through the kernel)⟩

**D-62 · A `∇` line puts the session in definition mode** until a line that is exactly `∇`. Definition-mode lines are stored, not evaluated; a syntax error in a body is reported at the closing `∇` with the body line number. ⟨`atl/readme-green`⟩

**D-69 · A line whose braces do not close continues on the next line** until they do; the lines are one statement, and a line end inside the braces separates statements like `⋄`. The held lines print nothing. A host that reads an open brace to its close itself (jojo does) hands the kernel the lines as one entry with line ends in it, and the lexer reads a line end as that separator; the session does the same for lines that arrive one at a time, as a transcript's do. A `⍝` comment ends at the line end. A lexing refusal in a continuation line is reported at once and the whole is dropped; a parse refusal is reported when the braces close; a body line's refusal names its line, at the call (D-67). The mode of the first line (D-54) is the mode of the statement. ⟨`atl/dfn-lines` (`SIGN`, `CAESAR`, the green `FACT`)⟩
